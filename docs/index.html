Quick guide Everything you need in one minute

  • What it measures. Whether students who scored Well Below or Below on the beginning-of-year DIBELS benchmark are being progress monitored on schedule.
  • Who is counted. Students currently enrolled in grades K to 5 with one of those two levels, counted under their homeroom teacher.
  • What counts as tested. At least 1 progress monitoring probe dated inside the window.
  • The goal. 95% of students tested by the end of every window. Well Below students have 2-week windows and Below students have 4-week windows.
  • How to use it. Pick a level and window, click a grade, then a teacher, then a student.

How the drill-down works Four pages, one idea: start broad, click down to the student

1. Grades back to top

The first page compares grades. You see tiles for Tested, Not tested, Last window and the Next checkpoint, a row of checkpoint cards for the current window (each with its date and target), and a bar for each grade with its tested % and a status pill. Grades that are off track also get a badge.

Try it in this demo: the current window is Well Below window 5 (2026-10-27 to 2026-11-09). 48 of 154 students (31%) have a probe in it so far. Click any grade bar.

2. Teachers

Clicking a grade opens the teachers in that grade, sorted from the lowest tested % to the highest. Each row shows tracked, tested and not tested counts, a bar, and a status pill.

Try it in this demo: 0 of 18 teachers have reached 95%. The lowest is FakeTeacher-5B, Demo at 0% and the highest is FakeTeacher-KA, Demo at 57%. Click FakeTeacher-5B, Demo.

3. Students

Clicking a teacher opens their students. A box lists students not tested in the window, so you know exactly who needs a probe. The list also shows each student's last probe date and aimline pill, and a search box finds a student by name.

Try it in this demo: Alpha FAKE339 still needs a probe in Well Below window 5. Click the name.

4. Student detail

Clicking a student opens a one-page profile:

  • Probe run chart. One panel per measure: dots are scores, the dotted line is the aimline, and the green line is the goal.
  • Window history. Every window with its dates, number of probes, a tested status and an aimline pill.
  • Probe history. Every probe with its date, score, aimline value, how far above (+) or below (-) the aimline it was, the goal and a status.

Use the Back button to return to the list.

Behind the scenes, three click actions connect the pages: grade to teachers, teacher to students and student to detail. Each filters the next page to what you clicked. If a page looks empty, click an item on the page before it.

The controls Two selectors and one setting

ControlWhat it does
LevelWell Below (2-week windows) or Below (4-week windows). The two groups have different calendars.
Window"Current" shows today's window. Pick 1 to 8 to look back at a closed window, or ahead at one that has not opened.
Behind pointsHow many points under the checkpoint still counts as "Behind" instead of "Off track". Default 15.

Closed windows are judged against the final 95% target, not a checkpoint. Windows that have not opened show "Not started".

Statuses and colors The same colors are used on every page

StatusMeaning
Met targetThe window has closed and 95% or more of students were tested.
On trackThe window is open and the tested % is at or above today's checkpoint.
BehindUp to 15 points below today's checkpoint (15 is the default; the Behind points setting changes it).
Off trackMore than 15 points below today's checkpoint.
Missed targetThe window has closed and fewer than 95% of students were tested.
Not startedThe window has not opened yet, or no checkpoint has passed.

Checkpoints

Each window has checkpoints along the way, for example 40%, 70%, 90% and 95% of students tested by set dates. Today's checkpoint is the one most recently passed. A grade or teacher is compared with that number, not with the final 95%, until the window closes.

The checkpoint percentages in this demo are placeholders. A real school would set its own.

The aimline pills

Below the latest probe was under the aimline. At or Above it was on or over the line. No probe nothing was tested in that window. Not available the platform gave no aimline for that probe.

About the assessments What each score and level means

Benchmark: the beginning-of-year level (DIBELS 8, BOY)

Every student takes the benchmark at the start of the year. The result is a composite level: Well Below, Below, At or Above benchmark. Only Well Below and Below students are expected to be progress monitored, so only they appear on this dashboard. The level stays the same all year, even if the student later improves.

The levels come from the benchmark cut scores that Amplify publishes for each grade and time of year. This dashboard never recalculates them: it reads the level straight from the benchmark export.

Progress monitoring: short checks between benchmarks

A probe is a one-minute check. Teachers give probes more often to students who need more support. Which measures a student takes depends on the grade, as below.

Goal and aimline

  • Goal. The score the student is working toward by the next benchmark.
  • Aimline. A straight line from where the student started to the goal. It shows the score a student should have on any date to stay on pace.
  • Aimline status. Each probe is compared with the aimline on its date: Below or At or Above. Some probes have no aimline yet, shown as "Not available".

Windows

The school year is cut into windows. In this demo, Well Below students are expected to be probed in every 2-week window and Below students in every 4-week window. These lengths follow Amplify's progress monitoring fidelity guidance. A student is tested in a window with at least 1 probe in it.

Data lag. Probes show up after the app syncs, which can take a few days. The "Data as of" date at the top of the dashboard says how current the numbers are.

From downloads to dashboard

  1. Save each export in its own folder. Name files like source_schoolyear_window_pulldate (for example dibels_pm_2026-27_boy-moy_2026-10-26_1338.csv).
  2. Add a small testing calendar (windows and checkpoint targets) in Excel.
  3. Run the build script. It combines every progress monitoring export (a repeated probe is kept once, from the newest file), joins the three sources by student number, and writes the two files the dashboard reads.
  4. Check the output against the raw exports, refresh the Tableau data sources, and republish.
Privacy. Real student data should stay inside your school's systems. This public demo uses fake records only, in exactly the same format as the real files.
